Age and Timing of Decompressive Hemicraniectomy for Acute Ischemic Stroke: Insights from the National Inpatient Sample (2017–2022)

Analysis for the RCOP NIS Neuro-14

Author
Affiliation

Taimor Mohammed Khan

Dow University of Health Sciences

Preamble:

  • Reference Studies:

    • Dasenbrock et al., 2016
    • Dasenbrock et al., 2017

  • Study Objective: To evaluate in-hospital outcomes among adults undergoing decompressive hemicraniectomy (DHC) for acute ischemic stroke (AIS), stratified by age group and timing of surgery, using data from the National Inpatient Sample (2017–2022). Specifically, this study examines whether older age and delayed surgical decompression are independently associated with higher in-hospital mortality, worse discharge disposition, longer length of stay, and higher rates of procedural and medical complications in a contemporary U.S. inpatient cohort.

  • Data Source: Retrospective cross-sectional analysis using the National Inpatient Sample (NIS) from 2017 to 2022. The NIS, part of the Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project (HCUP), provides a stratified, nationally representative sample of U.S. inpatient hospitalizations across all payers and geographic regions.

  • Cohort Definition: Hospitalizations were included if they met the following criteria

    • Age ≥18 years

    • Non-elective admission

    • Primary or secondary diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke (AIS), identified using ICD-10-CM code: (I63.x)

    • Performance of decompressive hemicraniectomy (DHC), identified using ICD-10-PCS codes: 00N00ZZ, 00N70ZZ, 0NB00ZZ, 0NT10ZZ, 0NT30ZZ, 0NT40ZZ, 0NT50ZZ, 0NT60ZZ

    • Exclusions: Patients with concurrent diagnoses of head trauma, subarachnoid hemorrhage, intracranial tumors, arteriovenous malformation, venous sinus thrombosis, Moyamoya disease, intracranial abscess, cerebral arteritis, extra-axial hematoma, unruptured cerebral aneurysm, vertebral artery dissection, or vertebrobasilar infarction

  • Exposure of Interest:

    • Age group (18–50, 51–60, 61–70, >70 years)

    • Timing of DHC (Early ≤48 hrs vs. Delayed >48 hrs)

      Surgical timing was derived from the NIS PRDAYn variables, which record the number of days elapsed between hospital admission and each procedure, where admission day = 0. To identify the timing of DHC specifically, each of the 25 procedure day variables (PRDAY1 – PRDAY25) was evaluated against the corresponding procedure code slot (I10_PR1 – I10_PR25). The procedure day corresponding to the DHC code was extracted and used to classify timing as Early (≤1 day from admission) or Delayed (>1 day from admission).

  • Outcomes of Interest:

    • Primary Outcome:

      • In-hospital mortality

    • Secondary Outcomes:

      • Discharge disposition (Home vs. Non-home)

      • Length of stay (days)

      • Prolonged mechanical ventilation (>96 hrs)

      • Pneumonia

  • Covariates for Adjustment: All models adjusted for the following

    • Demographic Factors: Age group (categorical), sex, race/ethnicity

    • Socioeconomic Factors: Primary payer, ZIP code–based income quartile

    • Clinical Factors: Elixhauser comorbidity score, atrial fibrillation, carotid dissection, carotid stenosis, hypercoagulable state, hyperlipidemia, tobacco use

    • Hospital-Level Factors: Hospital region, hospital bed size, hospital location and teaching status

  • Statistical Methods:

    • Univariable Analysis:

      • Categorical variables: Compared using Pearson’s chi-square test with Rao & Scott adjustment

      • Continuous variables: Analyzed using the design-based Kruskal–Wallis test, a non-parametric alternative suitable for survey-weighted data

    • Multivariable Analysis:

      • Binary outcomes (in-hospital mortality, discharge disposition, prolonged mechanical ventilation, pneumonia): Survey-weighted logistic regression using the quasibinomial family

      • Continuous outcomes (length of stay): Survey-weighted linear regression using the gaussian family

      • Results reported as adjusted odds ratios (aORs) or beta coefficients with 95% confidence intervals

  • Software: All statistical analyses were performed using the R Statistical Language (Version 4.5.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), incorporating survey-weighted procedures via the survey package to account for the complex sampling design of NIS.
